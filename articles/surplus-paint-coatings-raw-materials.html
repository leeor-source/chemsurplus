Cutting Raw-Material Cost in Paints & Coatings with Surplus TiO2, Resins & Solvents

2026-05-30 · 6 min read

Titanium dioxide alone can be 20-30% of a coating's raw-material cost. Surplus pigments, resins and solvents are the fastest lever a formulator has on margin.

Where the cost hides in a coating

Pigments and resins dominate a coating's bill of materials, and titanium dioxide is the single biggest line - often 20-30% of raw-material cost. That concentration is exactly why surplus and discontinued-grade TiO2, off-spec resins and overstock solvents carry such a strong savings incentive: a small percentage off the most expensive inputs moves the whole formula's margin.

Formulation and color changes also strand perfectly good binders and pigments as 'orphan' inventory - a steady surplus stream for the next formulator.

What clears well

Acrylic and styrene-acrylic emulsions, bisphenol-A epoxy resins, TiO2 and iron-oxide pigments, and solvents (toluene, MEK, ethyl acetate, acetone) all move readily as surplus into primers, industrial and lower-tier lines where a single off-spec parameter doesn't matter.

Short-dated emulsions are a particular opportunity - usable well within shelf life by a higher-throughput shop, at a deep discount.

FAQ

Can I use off-spec TiO2 in paint?

Often yes - off-spec usually misses one prime parameter (e.g. a shade or oil-absorption tolerance) and performs well in primers and industrial coatings. Always check the COA against your formula.
