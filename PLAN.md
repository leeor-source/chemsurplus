# ChemSurplus — The Optimized Plan

*How the marketplace works, who's in it, how value flows, and how we win. Grounded in market research (sources at bottom). See [ecosystem.html](ecosystem.html) for the visual version.*

## 0. Thesis in one line
The surplus-chemical market is **large, fragmented, and undigitized** — usable chemistry is written off or incinerated because the holder can't find the next buyer fast enough. ChemSurplus is the **clean, industry-personalized layer over existing brokers** that normalizes their messy inventory, proves trust (SDS/COA/compliance), and facilitates the deal — monetizing the **trust + logistics flow**, not a fat commission.

## 1. The market (why this is real)
- Global chemical **distribution** market ≈ **$283–323B (2025)** → **~$530–553B by 2035** (~5.1% CAGR). Specialty chemicals are **~55%** of it — the highest-margin, **most surplus-prone** slice.
- **No clean TAM exists for the surplus segment** — *that's the opportunity*: it's unmeasured because it's scattered across brokers and waste streams. We size it bottom-up two ways:
  1. **Disposal-cost-avoidance (the seller's quantified pain):** hazardous disposal runs **$200–$1,000+/ton** (+ **$146–$381/drum** treatment + **$45–$200/drum** transport). Diverting a ton to resale is a **$500–$2,500/ton economic swing** vs. destroying it.
  2. **Distribution market × specialty share × surplus rate.**
- Tailwinds: **circular economy / "beneficial reuse,"** inventory write-down pressure, **ESG** (landfill = reputational cost), and tax treatment (cash recovery vs. write-off).
- Timing: only **~1% of chemical transactions are online today**, but **85% of B2B buyers prefer digital for known products** and Dow already does **~40% of revenue digitally** — early, but the curve is bending.

## 2. The ecosystem — who's who

### Supply side — who holds surplus & why they sell
| Seller | Why they sell | Value / volume |
|---|---|---|
| **Manufacturers / producers** | Off-spec batches, overproduction, discontinued SKUs, ESG | **Highest value** — large, near-prime off-spec lots |
| **Distributors** | Aged/dead stock tying up capital & racking | High volume, mixed grade |
| **Formulators** (coatings, cleaning, personal care) | Reformulation orphans raw materials; over-ordering | **Highest relevance** — exactly what *other* formulators buy |
| **Plant closures / decommissioning** | One-time liquidation of full inventory + reagents | Lumpy, high-value, time-pressured |
| **Contract labs / pharma** | Expired-but-usable reagents, project-end surplus | Small lots, high unit value, doc-heavy |
| **Over-orderers / project remainders** | Cash recovery, space | Long-tail, frequent |
| **Brokers** *(our actual onboarding target)* | Want demand reach for inventory they already hold | **This is our cold-start supply** |

> In our model the **immediate seller is the broker.** Manufacturers/distributors/formulators are the *upstream* the brokers serve. We onboard brokers' catalogs as cheap, ready supply.

### Demand side — who buys what, and how spec-tolerant they are
The decisive axis is **grade**. Surplus/off-spec clears into the **technical-grade-tolerant lane**; prime/certified buyers (pharma, food, cosmetics) only take the *prime-surplus* (overstock-of-certified) sub-segment.

| Industry | Buys | Surplus tolerance | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Water treatment** | acids/bases, coagulants, commodity | **Very high** — price-driven | 🎯 Launch |
| **Paints & coatings** | resins/coatings, solvents, pigments, additives | **High** — classic surplus buyer | 🎯 Launch |
| **Cleaning / detergents** | surfactants, acids/bases, solvents | **High** | 🎯 Launch |
| **Plastics / polymers** | polymers, additives, intermediates | **High** — off-spec resin widely reused | 🎯 Launch |
| **Adhesives / sealants** | resins, polymers, solvents | **High** | Phase 2 |
| **Oil & gas** | solvents, acids, commodity, intermediates | **High** — volume/price-driven | Phase 2 |
| **Personal care / cosmetics** | surfactants, specialty actives, solvents | **Low–medium** | Phase 3 (prime-surplus) |
| **Food & beverage** | acids/bases, additives | **Low** — food-grade/NSF | Phase 3 (prime-surplus) |
| **Pharma / labs** | reagents, intermediates, solvents | **Low** — GMP/COA | Phase 3 (prime-surplus) |

**Strategic read:** our "pick your industry" personalization is most valuable in the **high-tolerance lane**. **Lead GTM with water treatment, coatings, cleaning, plastics** — large, price-driven, and exactly matched to the surplus formulators and distributors dump.

## 3. The economy — how value flows
```
Manufacturers ─┐
Distributors  ─┼─▶  BROKERS (hold/source inventory)  ─▶  CHEMSURPLUS  ─▶  BUYERS (by industry)
Formulators   ─┘     [our supply we onboard]            [interface, trust,    [pay below virgin,
Plant closures                                            matching, logistics]   documented, fast]
```
- **Brokers already capture the virgin-vs-surplus spread.** A fat commission on top is hard — channel margin is finite and brokers will defend it.
- So we capture value where we **add** value: **normalization, trust, matching, and logistics/payments** — recurring and disintermediation-resistant.

## 4. Facilitation model (the trust layer IS the product)
What it takes to close a hazmat chemical deal online — each is product surface and a monetization hook:
- **Documentation, normalized:** SDS (shipping-mandatory), **COA** (batch/grade proof — the credibility anchor for off-spec), TDS. Normalizing these across messy brokers is the core moat.
- **Verification & badges:** verified broker, COA-backed, grade-certified — shown consistently (already in the UI) and usable as filters.
- **Compliance automation:** **TSCA** (import + inventory check per ingredient), **REACH** (EU), **DOT/IATA** hazmat. Penalties up to **$84,425/violation/day** — automating this is real value, not box-ticking.
- **Hazmat logistics & freight:** proper BoL, UN number, ERG — trust *and* a logistics markup.
- **Payments / escrow:** real-time landed cost, float, dispute resolution, QA/returns.

## 5. Monetization — chosen to survive disintermediation
Disintermediation is the **#1 structural risk** (analogous marketplaces lose **30–80%** of revenue to off-platform deals). The model is built to be paid *before/around* the spot transaction:

1. **Broker SaaS + lead-gen (primary).** Knowde-style **seller-pays subscription**: normalized storefronts, COA/SDS automation, qualified RFQ leads. Recurring, partner-aligning, leakage-resistant. *(Knowde raised $91M on exactly this — subscription, buyers free.)*
2. **Modest transaction fee 3–8%** on deals we **fully facilitate** (payment + logistics) — justified because surplus chemicals are *hard to describe/compare* (the Xometry "harder good = higher take" rule), capped by broker tolerance. (Pure-liquidation analogs take 15–25%, but they own the asset; we don't.)
3. **Logistics + payments markup** — hazmat freight, escrow float, financing, featured listings. **This is also the moat** (the "liquidity moat": own fulfillment → become the default channel).

**Net:** durable revenue = **subscription + logistics/payments**, with commission as upside on fully-facilitated deals — *not* a pure take-rate play.

## 6. Liquidity & go-to-market (sequenced, not both-sides-at-once)
- **Supply-aggregated, demand-led.** Unlike typical B2B (demand-first), we can **bootstrap supply cheaply** by onboarding existing broker catalogs, then spend the real effort on **demand**.
- **Single-player utility first:** give brokers a normalized-listing / SDS-management tool that's useful *before* buyers arrive — that's how we onboard messy inventory without waiting for demand.
- **Per-industry liquidity milestones:** target **~500 closed transactions per industry** (the vertical-marketplace "default channel" flip-point) before expanding — start in the spec-tolerant lane.
- **Demand wedge:** the industry-personalized experience + buyer-relevant SEO (already built: 13 keyword pages, 9 industry pages).

## 7. Moat (ranked by durability)
1. **Data normalization across messy brokers** — strongest; compounds with every broker onboarded.
2. **Trust / compliance layer** — high switching cost once in the workflow.
3. **Logistics ("liquidity moat")** — owning fulfillment makes us the default channel.
4. **Industry personalization** — a UX wedge, not the moat (copyable).
> Caveat: network effects are **weak** here (buyers need only a handful of sellers) — lean on data + trust + logistics, not classic network effects.

## 8. Risks & mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Disintermediation / leakage (30–80%)** | Withhold broker identity until commit; own payments + logistics; make SaaS/compliance indispensable |
| **Channel conflict** (brokers fear commoditization) | Frame as demand-gen, not competition; seller-pays aligns incentives |
| **Regulatory / liability** (wrong grade into pharma/food) | Hard industry verification gates; compliance automation |
| **Misrepresentation / COA fraud** | Verification badges, sampling, returns policy |
| **Slow adoption (~1% online)** | Value-add (compliance, logistics, trust) strong enough to pull a relationship-driven industry online |

## 9. Roadmap
- **Phase 0 — Now (live):** SEO-led storefront, industry personalization, lot detail + docs/trust, RFQ flow, product imagery, serverless RFQ backend. *(shipped)*
- **Phase 1 — Supply onboarding:** broker listing/normalization tool (CSV import + SDS/COA parse), verified-broker profiles, RFQ routing to brokers + email. *(backend scaffolded — `api/`)*
- **Phase 2 — Facilitate the deal:** payments/escrow, hazmat freight quotes, compliance checks (TSCA/REACH), buyer & seller dashboards with status.
- **Phase 3 — Monetize & scale:** broker subscriptions, transaction fee on facilitated deals, expand to Phase-2/3 industries (prime-surplus for pharma/food/cosmetics).

## 10. North-star & KPIs
- **North star:** facilitated GMV (and tons diverted from disposal — the ESG story).
- **Liquidity:** closed transactions per industry (→ 500 flip-point); RFQ→quote→won conversion; time-to-first-quote.
- **Supply:** brokers onboarded; % listings with COA; catalog freshness.
- **Demand:** industry-qualified RFQs; repeat buyers; SEO impressions→RFQ.
- **Unit economics:** take rate on facilitated deals; subscription MRR; logistics margin; leakage rate.
